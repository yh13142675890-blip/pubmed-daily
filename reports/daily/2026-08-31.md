PURPOSE: This study uses original data from a 10-year cohort analysis of diagnosis, imaging, and treatment pathways in pediatric vascular anomalies at a specialized interdisciplinary center. The study examines the role of radiology in establishing diagnostic and therapeutic algorithms for interdisciplinary management of children with vascular anomalies.
MATERIAL AND METHODS: A total of 331 children were included in this study. Patient characteristics, the location, and anatomical site of the vascular anomaly and associated pain levels were evaluated on a dedicated questionnaire. Diagnostic procedures (imaging, biopsies) and therapy recommendations (conventional compression therapy, anticoagulation, minimally invasive interventional procedures and surgery) were also analyzed. The more familiar physicians and especially pediatric specialists become with them, the more patient care can improve. The contribution of radiology to provide comprehensive care for these patients includes imaging, minimally invasive therapies, and long-term follow-ups.
KEY POINTS: · vascular anomalies represent a rare disease. · holistic treatment requires a dedicated interdisciplinary center. · dedicated training for the specialties involved is necessary. · children with vascular anomalies have complex conditions that have to be addressed.

We report three children illustrating endoscopic, surgical, and medical management. All presented with melena and transfusion-requiring anemia. Case 1 developed gastric perforation after endoscopic band ligation and underwent emergency primary repair with bowel-preserving excision of accessible lesions, followed by recovery. We aimed to systematically characterize the angioarchitecture of TDS in bAVMs, focusing on whether transdural feeders terminated within the nidus or connected directly to the draining vein. TDS prevalence and its associated clinical features were also investigated.
METHODS: This retrospective study enrolled 521 patients (524 bAVMs) from 16 centers who underwent systematic six-vessel digital subtraction angiography. bAVMs were classified as nidus- or fistula-dominant. METHODS: A systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted of all English-language publications reporting clinical outcomes after combined embolization and surgical resection for AVMs. Single- and 2-arm analyses were performed using random-effects modeling.
