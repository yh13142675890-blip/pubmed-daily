#### 摘要

The benefits of surgical resection of unruptured cerebral arteriovenous malformations (AVMs) remains controversial. In 2014, The ARUBA (A Randomized trial of Unruptured Brain Arteriovenous Malformations) trial reported worse outcomes with the interventional treatment strategies used in the trial than with medical management alone. #### 摘要

Extracranial arteriovenous malformations (eAVMs) are congenital high-flow vascular anomalies characterized by direct connections between arteries and veins, resulting in progressive lesion growth, recurrent bleeding, pain, and significant morbidity. Recent studies suggest that somatic mutations in endothelial cells (ECs), most commonly in MAP2K1 and KRAS, initiate eAVM formation. However, these mutations alone cannot fully explain the abnormal vascular architecture, vessel instability, or frequent recurrence after treatment. Mural cells (MCs), including pericytes (PCs) and vascular smooth muscle cells (vSMCs), may also contribute to eAVM progression, although their role remains unclear. Under normal conditions, ECs and MCs communicate through signaling pathways such as PDGFB-PDGFRβ, Ang1-Tie2, Notch3, and TGF-β to regulate MC recruitment, vessel maturation, barrier integrity, vascular tone, and extracellular matrix homeostasis. Emerging therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring EC-MC communication and vascular stability are also reviewed. The possible contribution of MCs to eAVM pathogenesis provides a broader view of disease biology. However, whether therapies targeting both EC abnormalities and MC dysfunction can improve long-term vascular stability and reduce lesion recurrence remains to be determined.

Thoracic outlet syndrome results from the compression of neurovascular structures as they pass through the thoracic outlet, often leading to neurogenic symptoms involving the brachial plexus. We present two cases of young adults with persistent upper arm and hand symptoms due to brachial plexus compression associated with aberrant arteries. Despite nonsurgical management, including physical therapy and anti-inflammatory medications, both patients continued to experience disabling symptoms. Surgical exploration has revealed previously undescribed anomalous arterial branches deep to the anterior scalene muscle and crossing directly over the C7-T1 nerve roots.
