#### 摘要

Background The surgical management of cerebral arteriovenous malformations (AVMs) requires a meticulous balance between complete nidus eradication and the preservation of adjacent normal physiological structures. While the elimination of true malformed, nidal-draining veins is mandatory to avoid intraoperative rupture, sacrificing adjacent, non-malformed, medial cortical, and deep venous channels can cause localized venous congestion, tissue infarction, and poor functional outcomes. Objective This study analyzed the clinical, radiological, and functional outcomes of a deliberate microsurgical strategy focused on preserving critical medial venous pathways during AVM resection in an early patient series. Materials and methods A retrospective study was conducted on a consecutive series of 16 adult patients (aged >18 years) who underwent micro-neurosurgical resection of cerebral AVMs (Spetzler-Martin grades I-IV) at a single private academic institution between 2018 and 2025. Conclusions In this early clinical series, prioritizing the integrity of non-malformed medial veins during cerebral AVM surgery was a safe, effective, and anatomically sound strategy. #### 摘要

Brain arteriovenous malformations (AVMs) are complex and heterogeneous lesions that can rupture, causing significant morbidity and mortality. While ruptured lesions are usually treated, the management of unruptured AVMs remains unclear. A Randomized trial of Unruptured Brain Arteriovenous Malformations (ARUBA) was the first trial conducted to compare the effects of medical and interventional therapy. Although it concluded that medical therapy was superior in preventing stroke and death over a follow-up period of 33 months, the findings were met with intense criticism regarding several aspects of study design, progression, and analysis/conclusion. Namely, the increased use of stand-alone embolisation relative to microsurgery in a cohort with predominantly low-grade lesions combined with a short follow-up period amplified treatment risk. These reports showed that favourable safety profiles and cure rates can be achieved with appropriate patient selection and judicious use of different treatment modalities in multidisciplinary centres. Since large prospective randomised trials on AVMs may not be feasible, it is important to make use of practice-based data beyond the flawed ARUBA study to optimise patients' lifetime outcomes.

OBJECTIVE: Tinnitus is the perception of sound without an external source. More than 50 million people in the United States have reported experiencing tinnitus, resulting in an estimated prevalence of 10% to 15% in adults. The focus of this guideline is on tinnitus that is both bothersome and persistent (lasting 6 months or longer), which often negatively affects the patient's quality of life. The target audience for the guideline is any clinician, including allied health professionals, involved in managing patients with tinnitus. The target patient population is limited to adults (18 years and older) with tinnitus that is persistent and bothersome.
PURPOSE: The purpose of this guideline is to provide and update evidence-based recommendations for clinicians managing patients with tinnitus. The target audience for this guideline is any clinician involved in managing patients with tinnitus. This updated guideline provides clinicians with a logical framework to improve patient care and mitigate the personal and social effects of persistent, bothersome tinnitus. The first part of the guideline is focused on the clinical evaluation of patients with tinnitus, including the history, physical exam, and selection and timing of diagnostic testing and specialty referral to identify potential underlying treatable pathology and reduce the risk of self-harm. Clinicians should obtain a comprehensive audiological evaluation in patients with tinnitus (KAS 5). Clinicians should educate patients with tinnitus about the natural history and effects of, and management strategies for tinnitus (KAS 7). Clinicians may recommend sound enrichment to patients with persistent, bothersome tinnitus (KAS 10). Clinicians may offer bimodal neuromodulation to patients with persistent, bothersome tinnitus (KAS 14B).

DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: Phase 2, investigator-blinded, multi-institutional RCT conducted in 3 academic vascular anomalies clinics on 19 of 44 eligible infants aged between 2 weeks and 6 months.
