#### 摘要

PURPOSE: Because the best possible treatment for venous malformations is unclear, this study systematically reviews the available literature regarding the effectiveness of different treatment options for the patient group. Venous malformations result from incorrect development of the veins during embryogenesis and are present at birth. Venous malformations may exhibit symptoms, such as pain, swelling, and inflammation of the vessel.
MATERIALS AND METHODS: A systematic literature search in PubMed and Embase was performed. Data regarding the design, participants, intervention and, treatment outcome (success and complications) were extracted. The validity of the studies was assessed with the Cochrane Collaboration's risk of bias tool.

#### 摘要

Arteriovenous malformations (AVMs) are generally considered congenital vascular anomalies, whereas postoperative iatrogenic AVMs and arteriovenous fistulas (AVFs) are rare. In colorectal surgery, newly detected aneurysm-like mesenteric vascular lesions during postoperative surveillance may be difficult to distinguish from aneurysms, pseudoaneurysms, AVMs, and AVFs. A 75-year-old woman underwent laparoscopic right hemicolectomy for transverse colon cancer. Preoperative contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) showed no vascular malformation or aneurysmal lesion. Six months after surgery, surveillance CT revealed a 3.8 mm aneurysm-like lesion in a peripheral branch of the superior mesenteric artery near the anastomotic site. The lesion was clinically diagnosed as an ileal AVM, although acquired AVF remained an important differential diagnosis. Transcatheter embolization using n-butyl cyanoacrylate was successful. This case suggests that AVM or AVF should be considered in the differential diagnosis of aneurysm-like mesenteric vascular lesions detected after colorectal surgery.

#### 摘要

Inflammation and coagulation are tightly interconnected host-defense systems that, when dysregulated, promote pathological thrombosis. This narrative review examines mechanisms, clinical settings, and therapeutic implications of thromboinflammation (TI) across venous, arterial, and microvascular thrombosis, with particular attention to the differences in clinical translation among vascular territories. Endothelial activation, tissue factor expression, platelet-leukocyte interactions, neutrophil extracellular traps, complement activation, inflammasome signalling, and impaired fibrinolysis converge to generate a procoagulant phenotype. By contrast, the role of inflammation-targeted therapies for primary or secondary prevention of VTE remains uncertain. Future studies should identify disease-specific biomarkers and test targeted anti-inflammatory or immunothrombotic strategies, alone or alongside anticoagulation, to prevent thrombosis without compromising haemostasis effectively.

It further discusses criteria for patient selection and clinical decision-making regarding the introduction of anti-inflammatory therapy in individuals with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.
