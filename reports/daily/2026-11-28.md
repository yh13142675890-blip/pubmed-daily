BACKGROUND: Hemorrhagic conversion is the most devastating complication of brain arteriovenous malformations (bAVMs). However, endothelial mechanisms promoting macrophages (Mϕ) recruitment, microglia (MG) activation, and bAVM lesion instability remain unclear. We investigated vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 (VCAM1) as a mediator linking oncogenic endothelial signaling to myeloid cell responses and hemorrhagic progression in bAVMs.
METHODS: We performed integrated transcriptomic analysis of human bAVM and epilepsy-control brain tissues, with validation in local bAVM specimens. Transcriptomic and proteomic analyses of bAVM lesions and intact brain tissues from mutant KRAS-induced bAVM mice were followed by histological validation. Using AAV-BR1-KRAS G12V -injected Cx3cr1-gfp;Ccr2-rfp mice, we performed intravital imaging to assess Mϕ/MG responses to VCAM1 neutralization. Hemorrhage and myeloid cell responses were also evaluated following endothelial-specific VCAM1 deletion . Plasma soluble VCAM1 (sVCAM1) was measured in patients with unruptured and ruptured bAVMs and unruptured aneurysm controls. Effects of sVCAM1 on MG activation were examined in vitro .
RESULTS: Integrated human endothelial bulk and single-cell RNA-sequencing and tissue validation identified VCAM1 upregulation in bAVMs, including unruptured lesions. Plasma analyses showed increased sVCAM1 in unruptured and ruptured bAVM patients compared with unruptured aneurysm controls. In mutant KRAS-induced bAVM mice, transcriptomic and proteomic analyses demonstrated increased VCAM1 expression, with endothelial localization confirmed histologically. In vitro, mutant KRAS increased sVCAM1 production, while recombinant sVCAM1 stimulated inflammatory mediator expression in MG. Both MG and Mϕ expressed VLA-4, a major VCAM1 receptor. Intravital imaging showed that VCAM1 neutralization reduced Mϕ accumulation and MG activation, while early VCAM1 neutralization reduced hemorrhagic burden. Consistently, endothelial-specific VCAM1 deletion reduced Mϕ accumulation, MG activation, and hemorrhage volume in bAVM mice and improved behavioral performance.
CONCLUSIONS: These findings identify endothelial VCAM1 as an upstream mediator linking mutant KRAS signaling to myeloid cell responses and hemorrhagic progression in bAVMs. This study establishes endothelial VCAM1 as a potential therapeutic target for attenuating hemorrhagic progression.

These complementary loss-of-function approaches establish endothelial VCAM1 as a functional mediator of inflammatory bAVM instability.
SUMMARY OF NOVELTY AND SIGNIFICANCE: Hemorrhagic conversion is the most devastating complication of bAVMs, yet the endothelial mechanisms that initiate inflammatory responses and promote bAVM lesion instability remain poorly understood. Together, these findings establish endothelial VCAM1 as a functional mediator of the inflammatory axis connecting oncogenic vascular signaling to myeloid cell responses and hemorrhagic instability in bAVMs. This study identifies VCAM1 as a potential therapeutic target for limiting hemorrhagic progression and provide a mechanistic framework for understanding how oncogenic endothelial signaling contributes to inflammatory vascular dysfunction beyond bAVMs.

#### 摘要

OBJECTIVES: This prospective study aims to evaluate the safety and clinical outcomes of bleomycin electroembolotherapy (BEET) for treating extracranial arteriovenous malformations (AVMs).
MATERIALS AND METHODS: This represents a subanalysis of the prospective multicenter APOLLON trial (Clinical trial register, DRKS00021019). Children and adults with symptomatic AVMs unsuitable for or refractory to conventional treatments underwent BEET at two specialized vascular anomaly centers. Clinical and imaging findings were assessed at baseline and at 6-month follow-up intervals to evaluate subjective outcome (symptom-free, partial relief, no improvement, clinical progression) and AVM lesion devascularization on MRI (total, 100%; substantial, 76-99%; partial, 51-75%; slight, 50%; progression). Minor and major complications were assessed according to the CIRSE classification system.
RESULTS: Thirty-eight patients with symptomatic AVMs, mostly of fine-fistulous type (Cho IIIa, 25/38, 65.8%) and located in the face (20/38, 52.6%), lower (12/38, 31.6%) or upper extremities (6/38, 15.8%), underwent 84 procedures. The mean number of procedures per patient was 2.2 ± 1.5. Mean dose of the bleomycin was 8.3 ± 5.0 mg per session. Twenty-eight patients (73.7%) showed partial relief of symptoms, 9/38 (23.7%) patients were symptom-free, and 1/38 (2.6%) showed no improvement of symptoms. MRI was available in 34/38 (89.5%) patients. Substantial devascularization (76-99%) was assessed in 18/34 (52.9%), partial devascularization (51-75%) in 10/34 (29.4%), and complete devascularization in 4/34 (11.8%) patients, while slight devascularization was achieved in 2/34 (5.9%) patients. There was one major complication, a post-therapeutic ulceration of the lip managed with debridement and defect reconstruction (1/84, 1.2%; grade 3).
CONCLUSION: BEET demonstrated promising short-term clinical and imaging results, with a low rate of major complications. Notably, favorable outcomes were observed in predominantly complex cases, such as fine-fistulous lesions and challenging locations like the face and distal extremities.
KEY POINTS: Question Current treatment of extracranial arteriovenous malformations (AVMs) remains challenging, particularly in therapy-refractory and difficult-to-treat lesions, requiring effective minimally invasive alternatives. Findings Bleomycin electroembolotherapy (BEET) achieved symptom improvement in 97.4% of patients with AVMs in the face or distal extremities, substantial/complete devascularization in 64.7%, with one major complication. Clinical relevance BEET represents a promising minimally invasive treatment option for AVMs, providing meaningful symptom relief and lesion devascularization with an acceptable safety profile, including challenging fine-fistulous lesions and anatomically difficult regions.

#### 摘要

Zebrafish (Danio rerio) have emerged as a leading vertebrate model for cardiovascular research, offering unique advantages such as optical transparency, genetic tractability, and high fecundity. This review provides a comprehensive synthesis of the roles of hemodynamic forces, including wall shear stress (WSS), pressure gradients, and the oscillatory shear index (OSI), in regulating cardiovascular development, homeostasis, and disease pathogenesis. We describe the integration of advanced in vivo imaging modalities, such as light-sheet microscopy, high-frequency ultrasound, and high-speed particle-tracking velocimetry, with computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and fluid-structure interaction (FSI) models. These synergistic approaches enable precise quantification of biomechanical parameters and elucidation of the mechanobiological signaling pathways that transduce hemodynamic stimuli into cellular responses, particularly the Notch and Wnt cascades. The utility of zebrafish in modeling congenital heart defects, cardiomyopathies, vascular malformations, and vascular metabolic diseases is also highlighted, including diabetic retinopathy, thrombosis, and hyperlipidemia, alongside their critical role in high-throughput drug screening and mechanistic studies of cardioprotective agents. Finally, we address current limitations related to anatomical differences and scaling effects, and propose future directions in multi-omics integration, patient-specific modeling, and artificial intelligence (AI)-driven analytics to enhance translational relevance.

C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 12 (CXCL12), C-X-C chemokine receptor type 4 (CXCR4), and atypical chemokine receptor 3 (ACKR3; formerly CXCR7) regulate leukocyte recruitment, vascular homeostasis, platelet activation, chemokine availability, and tissue repair. This narrative review examines their context-dependent roles in atherosclerosis and ischemic stroke. Experimental ischemia models inform injury and repair without establishing an atherosclerotic cause. Most vascular mechanistic evidence derives from extracranial human samples and experimental models; direct functional validation in human intracranial plaques remains limited. Human circulating-biomarker associations and exploratory CXCR4-targeted imaging do not yet establish clinical prediction or treatment benefit. Further translation requires standardized biomarker methods, prospective imaging validation, and interventions that distinguish local target effects from systemic cell redistribution.

#### 摘要

BACKGROUND: Post-traumatic headache (PTH) is the most common and disabling sequela of mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI). For headache specialists, this population presents a unique clinical challenge: PTH is frequently refractory to conventional prophylaxis and is typically accompanied by a broader constellation of persistent post-concussion symptoms (PPCS), including vestibular, autonomic, and cognitive disturbances. However, psychological factors-specifically pain catastrophizing and fear-avoidance behaviors-are associated with symptom persistence and may interact with ongoing biological dysfunction.

We propose that these factors may act as 'amplifiers' that interact with central sensitization, contributing to the transition from an acute post-traumatic state to a chronic pain condition.
CONCLUSIONS: Successfully managing refractory PTH requires moving beyond isolated headache prophylaxis. Neurologists must adopt an interdisciplinary paradigm that simultaneously addresses the biological PPCS triad (vestibular, autonomic, cognitive) and the psychological factors associated with persistent symptoms.

#### 摘要

Sulfur-containing amino acids, particularly methionine, play a central role in brain metabolism and the maintenance of neural homeostasis. Perturbations in brain methionine metabolism have been associated with a wide spectrum of behavioral and neuropsychiatric outcomes, including anxiety- and depression-like phenotypes, as well as cognitive and behavioral impairments relevant to schizophrenia, fear processing, and bipolar disorder. In addition, brain methionine metabolism intersects with multiple molecular pathways implicated in major neurodegenerative diseases, such as Alzheimer's disease, multiple sclerosis, and Parkinson's disease. Accumulating evidence over the past two decades indicates that methionine intake and the integrity of the methionine metabolic network constitute a critical regulatory axis in brain health, capable of exerting both neuroprotective and neurotoxic effects in a context-dependent manner. Moderate dietary methionine restriction induces adaptive metabolic reprogramming in the brain, enhancing learning, memory, adult neurogenesis, and synaptic resilience, while simultaneously strengthening antioxidant defenses and resistance to oxidative stress. This review summarizes current research on dietary methionine restriction and its effects on neurological function, psychiatric conditions, and neurodegenerative diseases, with a particular focus on the underlying molecular mechanisms.

Cardiac fibroblasts activate to myofibroblasts in response to the inflammatory cytokine transforming growth factor beta1 (TGF-β1) to promote fibrotic scarring. Biological sex also influences cardiac fibrosis progression and patient outcomes, where males exhibit increased fibrotic scarring after acute inflammation relative to females. At the cellular level, sex differences in TGF-β1-mediated cardiac myofibroblast activation processes have not been clearly defined. We hypothesized that TGF-β1 would cause sex-specific cardiac myofibroblast activation levels and alter the secretion of bioactive molecules to modulate sex differences in cardiac fibrosis.
METHODS: Primary left ventricle cardiac fibroblasts were isolated from male and female C57BL/6 J mice and cultured on hydrogel biomaterials mimicking native myocardial ECM stiffness and treated with TGF-β1 and/or the TGF-β1 receptor inhibitor SD208. CONCLUSION: Our findings establish that TGF-β1 mediates sex differences in cardiac myofibroblast activation on hydrogels and secreted factors that may modulate the myocardial microenvironment. Our work underscores the importance of using hydrogels as cell culture platforms to recapitulate sex-specific cardiac fibrosis phenotypes as a steppingstone towards identifying sex-dependent therapeutic interventions for cardiac fibrosis.
