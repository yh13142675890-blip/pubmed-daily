#### 摘要

OBJECTIVE: To determine the efficacy of sclerotherapy for venous malformations (VMs).
METHODS: PubMed was used to search the medical literatures for publication on the combined topics of "VMs and sclerotherapy." The final evaluation of the venous lesions was based on both objective parameters and subjective parameters. Complications were also recorded.
RESULTS: A total of 35 studies published between 1986 and 2011 matched the selection criteria and were included. Ethanol, polidocanol, ethanolamine oleate, and sodium tetradecyl sulfate (STS) are the 4 mainstream sclerosants. The total efficiency of these 4 sclerosants all exceeded 90% except STS. CONCLUSIONS: Sclerotherapy is effective for VMs. However, there is limited evidence from randomized clinical trials to support the use of any kinds of sclerosants.

#### 摘要

Gastrointestinal arterio-venous malformations are a known cause of gastrointestinal bleeding. We present a rare case of persistent rectal bleeding due to a rectal arterio-portal venous fistula in the setting of portal hypertension secondary to portal vein thrombosis. The portal hypertension was initially surgically treated with splenectomy and a proximal splenorenal shunt. However, rectal bleeding persisted even after surgery, presenting us with a diagnostic dilemma. The patient was re-evaluated with a computed tomography mesenteric angiogram which revealed a rectal arterio-portal fistula. Arterio-portal fistulas are a known but rare cause of portal hypertension, and possibly the underlying cause of continued rectal bleeding in this case. Through this case report, we hope to highlight awareness of this unusual condition, discuss the diagnostic workup and our management approach.

#### 摘要

OBJECTIVE: In pediatric cerebral arteriovenous malformation (AVM), the long-term outcome data are scarce. The aim of this study was to analyze the clinical and radiological long-term outcomes from an institutional experience of children and adolescents treated for AVM over 25 years.
METHODS: The authors reviewed clinical and radiological data from a consecutive series of patients (age ≤ 18 years) treated for AVM at a single institution from 2000 to 2024, with at least 1 year of follow-up.
RESULTS: Overall, 96 patients were treated for AVM (48 males and 48 females, mean age 12.8 ± 4.8 years). Spetzler-Martin grades were I in 18 patients (19.4%), II in 33 (35.5%), and III in 30 (32.3%), and 14.6% of patients had an infratentorial location. Overall, 8.3% of patients were asymptomatic and 54.2% presented with hemorrhage. Moreover, 11.5% of patients experienced new or repeated hemorrhage during the initial observation or ongoing treatment. The stand-alone treatment was microsurgery in 38.5% of patients, radiosurgery in 28.1%, and embolization in 2.1%, while 9.4% of patients were treated conservatively. The treatment was multimodal in 21.9% and repeated in 49.4% of patients. The obliteration rate in patients with completed treatment (n = 75) was 80% (higher in those presenting with hemorrhage: 91.1% vs 63.3%, p = 0.003). The obliteration rate was 88.6% after microsurgery and 57.9% after radiosurgery (higher in the hemorrhagic group: 87.5% vs 36.4%, p = 0.026). Recurrence occurred in 8.3% of patients at a median of 60 months (range 36-68 months), and following microsurgery in all cases. A favorable clinical outcome (modified Rankin Scale scores 0-2) was achieved in 83.2% of patients, nonsignificantly less in those with hemorrhagic presentation (76.5% vs 90.9%, p = 0.061). The overall mortality rate was 5.3%, without a significant difference between the hemorrhagic and nonhemorrhagic groups. Hydrocephalus, hemorrhage volume, nidus size, infratentorial location, and Spetzler-Martin, supplementary, and combined grades were negative predictors of a good outcome in the univariate analysis, with the combined grade being the strongest predictor. Hemorrhage was a strong predictor of obliteration in the multivariate model, whereas radiosurgery and the number of treatments were predictors of nonobliteration.
CONCLUSIONS: Although more than half of children and adolescents with AVM presented with hemorrhage, most patients had a favorable clinical outcome. In our experience, microsurgery and radiosurgery resulted in the highest obliteration rates, particularly in hemorrhagic cases, while the role of endovascular treatment was only supplementary.

OBJECTIVE: To determine the rates of RIC after AVM SRS and identify risk factors.
METHODS: A literature review was performed using PubMed and MEDLINE to identify studies reporting RIC in AVM patients treated with SRS.
