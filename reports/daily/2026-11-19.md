- 发表日期: 2027 Feb 10

#### 摘要

ETHNOPHARMACOLOGICAL RELEVANCE: Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that causes memory loss, cognitive decline, and ultimately dementia. Available pharmacotherapies mainly provide symptomatic relief and do not adequately address the underlying pathology. Ganoderma lucidum (Curtis) P. Karst (G. lucidum) is a widely used medicinal mushroom rich in bioactive compounds, including polysaccharides, triterpenoids, and proteins. These constituents may alleviate the neuroinflammation and oxidative stress involved in AD pathogenesis.
AIM OF THE REVIEW: This review critically integrates evidence for the therapeutic effects of G. lucidum on AD-associated neuroinflammation and oxidative stress. It has three aims: (1) to examine how G. lucidum may disrupt the bidirectional vicious cycle between neuroinflammation and oxidative stress; (2) to compare the pharmacological profiles of different preparations and purified constituents; and (3) to evaluate AD-specific barriers to translation and inform future clinical development.
MATERIALS AND METHODS: We searched PubMed, Web of Science, Google Scholar, and CNKI for studies on G. lucidum and AD through 2026 using combinations of the primary term "Ganoderma lucidum" with "Alzheimer's disease," "neuroinflammation," "oxidative stress," and related terms. Included were original research, mechanism-based studies, and clinical evidence on G. lucidum bioactives in AD or related models. Evidence was stratified by design robustness and presented as a narrative synthesis.
RESULTS: The available evidence indicates that G. lucidum may act on several pathological processes associated with AD. Experimental studies report effects on abnormal amyloid-β (Aβ) aggregation, tau hyperphosphorylation, cholinergic dysfunction, neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and neuronal apoptosis. These findings illustrate the multi-target pharmacology of traditional Chinese medicines and help explain the potential activity of G. lucidum against AD.
CONCLUSIONS: G. lucidum has anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and neuroprotective activities that may be relevant to AD management. This review summarizes its reported effects on AD-related neuroinflammation and oxidative stress and examines the underlying mechanisms. Clinical translation remains limited by poor blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeability, uncertain pharmacokinetics, and non-standardized dosing. Rigorous preclinical studies and controlled early-phase trials are therefore required. Addressing these barriers may inform the potential translation of G. lucidum as an adjunctive strategy in multimodal AD therapy. We also identify key evidence gaps and propose a component-specific framework for future research.

- 发表日期: 2026 Dec
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jbo.2026.100815
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43036809/

#### 摘要

Myeloma bone disease develops in patients with a monoclonal expansion of malignant plasma cells in the bone marrow that induce osteoclast activity and suppress osteoblast function. Consequently, osteolytic bone lesions occur, causing fractures, pain, and limiting quality of life. Recent experimental, imaging, biomaterial, and translational studies indicate that multiple myeloma remodels the bone microenvironment at several interconnected levels: the extracellular matrix and matrisome, adhesion-dependent microenvironmental interactions, the immune contexture of the bone microenvironment, osteocyte lacunar architecture, bone material quality, mechanosensing, and the capacity for remineralization. Emerging evidence further suggests that endothelial adhesion molecules such as junctional adhesion molecule-A (JAM-A) and spatially organized immune-suppressive niches contribute to angiogenesis, tumor dissemination, immune escape, and persistence within osteolytic bone lesions. The emerging concept is that the myeloma bone niche is a structurally, mechanically, and molecularly altered ecosystem that both supports tumor growth and limits skeletal repair. In parallel, preclinical concepts suggest that the myeloma bone niche can be modulated by biophysical stimuli to induce skeletal repair in myeloma patients. Additionally, increasing evidence indicates that anti-myeloma therapies, including proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulatory agents, monoclonal antibodies, and B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA)-directed chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell approaches, not only control tumor burden but also modulate bone remodeling through direct effects on osteoclasts and osteoblasts as well as indirect reprogramming of osteoimmune interactions within the bone marrow microenvironment. While some of these therapies promote bone formation or reduce resorption, their overall impact on skeletal repair remains heterogeneous and incompletely understood.

#### 摘要

Dengue virus is a major mosquito-borne pathogen that causes clinical manifestations ranging from asymptomatic infection to severe dengue, including dengue hemorrhagic fever and dengue shock syndrome. Disease severity is strongly associated with antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE), in which pre-existing non-neutralizing antibodies promote Fcγ receptor-mediated viral entry into immune cells, resulting in enhanced viral replication, immune dysregulation, endothelial dysfunction, and vascular leakage. Emerging evidence indicates that microRNAs (miRNAs) are key post-transcriptional regulators of host immune responses, inflammatory signaling, and vascular integrity during dengue virus infection. This review summarizes current evidence on the interplay between ADE and miRNA-mediated regulation of dengue pathogenesis, with emphasis on innate and adaptive immunity, Fcγ receptor signaling, cytokine responses, and endothelial barrier dysfunction. We also highlight circulating and tissue-specific miRNAs with potential value as biomarkers for disease severity and prognosis, and discuss emerging miRNA-based therapeutic strategies, including miRNA mimics and inhibitors, as host-directed approaches to modulate ADE-associated pathways. Improved understanding of miRNA regulatory networks may facilitate the development of novel biomarkers and targeted therapeutic strategies for severe dengue.

#### 摘要

Sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI) is a major complication in the intensive care unit (ICU), associated with increased mortality and progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD) and end-stage renal disease (ESRD). This review summarizes current advances in the definition, epidemiology, pathophysiology, diagnosis, experimental modeling, and therapeutic strategies of SA-AKI, with emphasis on translational challenges. The conceptual framework of SA-AKI has evolved from global renal hypoperfusion to a multifactorial, predominantly non-ischemic process characterized by inflammatory dysregulation, microcirculatory dysfunction, and adaptive metabolic reprogramming. Tubular metabolic shutdown is increasingly recognized as a protective yet maladaptive response contributing to functional decline. Regulated cell death pathways, including pyroptosis and ferroptosis, further drive tubular injury. In parallel, organ crosstalk - particularly along the lung-gut-heart-kidney axis - amplifies renal dysfunction during sepsis. Recent diagnostic advances are highlighted, including the 2023 Acute Disease Quality Initiative (ADQI) consensus and emerging biomarkers reflecting distinct pathophysiological domains: tubular injury (neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin [NGAL] and kidney injury molecule-1 [KIM-1]), cell cycle arrest (Tissue Inhibitor of Metalloproteinases-2[TIMP-2]·insulin-like growth factor-binding protein 7 [IGFBP7]), and endothelial dysfunction (angiopoietin-2 [Angpt-2] and the tyrosine kinase with immunoglobulin-like and EGF-like domains 2 receptor [Tie2]), supporting early risk stratification beyond serum creatinine (sCr). Experimental models ranging from endotoxemia and cecal ligation and puncture to two-hit and comorbidity-based paradigms are critically appraised. Finally, current and investigational therapies - including hemodynamic optimization, extracorporeal cytokine removal, mitochondria-targeted interventions, and regenerative approaches - are discussed in the context of persistent barriers to clinical translation.

#### 摘要

ETHNOPHARMACOLOGICAL RELEVANCE: Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disorder characterized by progressive cognitive decline and multifactorial pathology. Because AD involves interacting pathological processes, the limited efficacy of conventional single-target therapies has increased interest in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) and other natural medicines with multi-target pharmacological actions. TCM-derived active components originate from medicinal materials used in traditional practice and are evaluated using modern pharmacological approaches. Their source medicines have diverse traditional-use backgrounds. Some have historical associations with memory or cognitive symptoms, whereas many others were traditionally used for broader deficiency, phlegm, blood-stasis, heat, or related syndromic patterns. These records provide ethnopharmacological context for the source medicines but should not be taken as historical evidence for the anti-AD activity of their isolated constituents.
AIM OF THE REVIEW: This review synthesizes current evidence on the mechanisms of TCM-derived bioactive components in AD while incorporating relevant traditional-use information on their source medicines. Particular attention is given to their multi-target actions and emerging mechanisms involving ferroptosis, mitophagy, and the gut-brain axis.
MATERIALS AND METHODS: Mechanistic literature published between January 2019 and November 2025 was retrieved from Ovid MEDLINE, PubMed, ScienceDirect, and Web of Science. Ovid MEDLINE and PubMed were searched separately to maximize retrieval across indexed and additional PubMed records, with overlapping records removed during deduplication. Traditional-use information was evaluated separately for the source medicines. A targeted search of PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov was also conducted to assess human interventional evidence for compounds covered in the review, with trial-registration information updated through August 2026.
RESULTS: Current preclinical findings suggest that TCM active components may produce coordinated preclinical pharmacological effects through multiple pathways, including the inhibition of β-amyloid (Aβ) production and deposition, regulation of tau aggregation and phosphorylation, alleviation of neuroinflammation and oxidative stress, improvement of mitophagy and iron metabolism disorders, and modulation of gut microbiota homeostasis. Among these components, saponins show broad mechanistic coverage. Flavonoids, polysaccharides, and other bioactive substances have likewise shown anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities, together with protective effects on synaptic function, in preclinical models. These mechanistic findings were derived predominantly from cellular and animal studies, with computational analyses providing mainly hypothesis-generating support. Human evidence was available for only a small subset of compounds and was not uniformly positive. Huperzine A has progressed to controlled clinical testing but has shown mixed efficacy findings; resveratrol demonstrated CNS exposure and biomarker activity without consistent cognitive benefit; curcumin did not show efficacy in a placebo-controlled AD trial; and favorable signals from ginseng studies were limited by open-label designs and the use of whole preparations rather than isolated ginsenosides.
CONCLUSIONS: The available evidence supports broad preclinical activity of TCM-derived components across several interconnected AD-related processes, but the strength of mechanistic support varies markedly among compounds and pathways. Human translation remains limited and inconsistent, and current clinical data do not establish disease-modifying efficacy for the compounds reviewed. Further development should therefore be guided by the main uncertainty surrounding each candidate-whether mechanistic causality, reproducibility, systemic or CNS exposure, preparation standardization, or clinical efficacy-rather than by a fixed sequence of technologies.

Continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) is traditionally used for solute and fluid removal in critically ill patients with acute kidney injury. Emerging evidence suggests that CRRT may also influence systemic biological networks, including inflammation, vascular function, immunity, and metabolism. In sepsis and critical illness, these systems are dynamically dysregulated, involving fluctuating inflammatory mediators, endothelial alterations, immune dysfunction ranging from hyperinflammation to immunoparalysis, and metabolic reprogramming that varies with disease stage. We propose a conceptual framework in which CRRT modulates these interconnected processes. Beyond solute and cytokine removal, CRRT may affect the distribution of circulating mediators, attenuate concentration peaks, influence endothelial stability, and modulate immune and metabolic states, thereby contributing to systemic network regulation.
